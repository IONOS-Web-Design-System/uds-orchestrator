# The hour in the brief is a fact about the photograph

The scene's `timeOfDay` is not a mood label. It is what time it is, and the light has to belong
to it. A frame is wrong when its hour and its light disagree, however good it looks otherwise.

- **Encode the light as the source that is actually there.** Name the specific thing the light is
  coming from at that hour — the window, the sun's height, the lamps that are switched on — and
  say what light-to-shadow relationship that source produces. Two things to avoid: a light generic
  enough to fit any hour, and a flat one with no shadow at all.
- **A dark hour stays dark.** Evening and night are dim, and their light comes from whatever is
  switched on indoors and from what little is left in the sky. Do not raise the exposure back up
  to a daytime frame because the room looks better bright — that is the single most common way an
  hour is lost. A lit lamp in a bright room is a daytime photograph with a lamp in it.
- **Every hour is available.** Nothing about any brand rules out an hour, a colour temperature or
  a key level. A late, low-lit room is as legitimate as a bright one when that is the hour the
  scene is set at; the brand lives in who is in the frame and how they hold themselves, not in
  the exposure.
- **Vary the hour across a set** rather than settling into one habitual look. This is a caution
  against repetition, not a preference for any particular hour.

Where a `Photographic lighting:` line IS injected it already made the light decision, including
the hour's part of it: follow it, and do not restate a light source of your own.
